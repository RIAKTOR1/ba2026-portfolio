Field 1 · Concept: Enterprise financial governance rests on the fundamental Balance Sheet Equation (Assets = Liabilities + Equity), partitioning resources and claims into short-term working capital cycles (≤12 months: cash, receivables, GAZelle parts inventory vs. 30–45 day vendor payables) and long-term structural solvency (racking/equipment vs. multi-year obligations) [1], [2]. Crucially, managers must separate accounting profit from real cash flow [2]: accrual profit recognizes revenue at invoice issuance, whereas cash flow tracks actual liquid inflows settling supplier bills. Diagnostic financial ratios (Current Ratio, Acid-Test, Net Profit Margin, Debt-to-Equity) standardize ledger signals [1], [2].
Concept Check: Why Automated AI Cannot Replace a Human Financial Auditor

Financial ratios offer directional benchmarks rather than self-executing verdicts because healthy thresholds depend on tax regimes, strategic inventory buffers, and cash-settlement speed. Automated AI suffers from contextual blindness: models evaluate static textbook cutoffs without observing local Kaspi QR cash velocity, EAEU border transit delays, or off-ledger B2B mechanic trust, making a human auditor indispensable.

"Theory of Constraints, Process Mapping, and Urgent B2B Counter Dispatch"

Concept Check: Why does automating a non-bottleneck station not increase total throughput?

Under the Theory of Constraints (Goldratt) and queuing theory, the maximum output of an entire linear system is strictly governed by the capacity of its slowest operational node (the bottleneck). Automating an upstream or downstream non-bottleneck simply produces work-in-progress (WIP) that piles up directly in front of the unaddressed constraint. Output rises only when the bottleneck itself is expanded or eliminated.

Concept Check: Can an AI system be accountable for an operational failure?

No. In managerial and legal theory, accountability requires legal agency, moral responsibility, and contractual liability—attributes exclusive to human managers [3]. When an algorithmic pricing rule fails or inventory runs out, liability rests entirely with the designated human owner who approved the system guardrails and fallback contingencies [3], [4].
